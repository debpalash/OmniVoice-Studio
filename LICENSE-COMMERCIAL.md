## Why a commercial license exists

OmniVoice Studio's source is offered under the **GNU Affero General Public License
v3 (AGPL-3.0-or-later)**. The AGPL is genuine, OSI-approved open source — but it
is **strong copyleft**: if you distribute OmniVoice, build it into another product,
or make it available to others over a network (including as a hosted/SaaS service),
the AGPL requires you to release the **complete corresponding source** of your
work under the AGPL too.

For many companies that is a non-starter. The **commercial license** lifts the
AGPL's copyleft/source-disclosure obligations so you can use OmniVoice in a
closed-source or proprietary setting.

## You likely need a commercial license if you want to…

- **Embed** OmniVoice (or a derivative) in a closed-source product you ship.
- **Redistribute** OmniVoice as part of a larger proprietary application.
- **Offer it as a hosted / SaaS / API service** to third parties without
  open-sourcing your service.
- Integrate it into a proprietary internal platform where AGPL's network-use
  clause would otherwise compel disclosure.

## You do NOT need a commercial license to…

- Use OmniVoice **as-is** as a desktop app (personal, educational, research,
  internal evaluation).
- Modify it for your own use, **as long as you comply with the AGPL** (i.e., you
  make your modified source available to anyone you convey it to or who interacts
  with it over a network).

> **Open question for the maintainer:** if the goal is to charge *all* business use
> — including a company using the unmodified desktop app purely internally — note
> that **neither AGPL nor a commercial dual-license compels that**, because pure
> internal use triggers no AGPL distribution/network obligation. Charging that
> cohort requires a *source-available* license (BUSL-1.1 / PolyForm Noncommercial)
> instead, which is **not** OSI open source. Pick the lever before finalizing.
